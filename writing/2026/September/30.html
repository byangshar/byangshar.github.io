The Shades of Change

Leaves,
bestowing a thorough veil,
yet giving light a way
to pass through somewhat,
and tame the rays
for the eye that catches the glare.

The conjoint effort has left but
a couple spaces for light to fall,
apparent when out on a stroll, 
or walking back home from work;
in a moment of odd luck,
out of those that succinctly pass,
as do days with age,
as fables rot and decay, and a reflection 
once bright, begins to fade.

It was suddenly evermore clear:
this one chance at life that came to be 
in the August-yield, in the backyard 
of a poor farmer’s labour’s home,
after 37 years of blight 
away from home,
past a mountain, and a couple more
that no one man could climb;
the winds and the lands carried grief
no man could describe,
one could but feel a knot in the chest
that went up into the arms
and put them to rest;
breathing soon came to halt, 
and the last tears that would come,
soon left the eyes.

There was no one around: 
to commend the passage of a day, 
or condemn hours that were swept away,
or wipe tears that never left the eyes, 
or spare me from domestic tasks for a while.
There was no one left:
some went on a stroll
and lost their way, 
some were kept under veils 
of hesitation and shame, 
some grew old in age
and couldn’t remember well or properly talk 
or endow command 
or function in accordance to god;
of which there was none,
as the hedon hid in faith’s facade, 
and dismembered all that he stood for.

This state of affairs aggravated
the long-settled monks that strayed away
from conflict, and furthered themselves from pain.
Yet all barriers break
when a bead frees from the chain
and tumbles down the path I came,
and is lost in long reeds in the way.

This I wish,
that a moment of odd luck never again comes,
and that I never doubt my disbelief in god,
and that all those lost return to my home, 
and that I tread someday, past the mountains up north,
and that I reach the lands of the crop sown 
37 years before my own,
and that nature has been kind to my abode,
and that everyday, there is a lamp lit by door
for my arrival…
